# One Health

## One-Sentence Definition

One Health is the recognition that human, animal, and environmental health are a single interconnected system, and that infectious disease problems must be addressed across all three.

## Simple Explanation

You cannot fix resistance or emerging infections in hospitals alone when the reservoirs are in farms, wildlife, and water.

## Detailed Scientific Explanation

**Two flagship problems**

1. **Zoonoses and emergence.** The majority of emerging human infectious diseases originate in animals. Drivers: land-use change, intensive livestock production, wildlife trade, and climate-driven vector range shifts. Examples span influenza reassortment in birds and swine, coronaviruses, Ebola, and vector-borne disease expansion.

2. **Antimicrobial resistance.** Antimicrobials used in food animals — historically for growth promotion — select for resistant organisms and mobile resistance genes that reach humans through food, direct contact, and the environment. Colistin resistance via the plasmid-borne *mcr-1* gene is the textbook case: detected in livestock first, then in human isolates worldwide.

**Environmental dimension:** wastewater and pharmaceutical manufacturing effluent create selection pressure outside any clinic; the environmental resistome is the reservoir from which clinical resistance genes are recruited ([[Horizontal Gene Transfer]]).

**Practical implementation:** integrated surveillance across sectors, shared genomic databases ([[Public Sequence Databases]]), restrictions on veterinary use of critically important antimicrobials, wastewater monitoring, and joint outbreak response between veterinary and public health authorities.

**Barriers:** separate ministries, separate budgets, separate data systems, and unequal capacity between countries ([[FAIR Data and Genomic Surveillance]]).

## Clinical Importance
- Exposure history — animal contact, travel, food, occupation — changes the differential diagnosis

## Research Importance
- Cross-sector genomic surveillance to trace resistance and pathogen flow between compartments
