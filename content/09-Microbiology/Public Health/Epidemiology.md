# Epidemiology

## One-Sentence Definition

Epidemiology is the study of how disease is distributed in populations and what determines that distribution.

## Simple Explanation

Who gets sick, where, when, and why — and what changes if we intervene.

## Detailed Scientific Explanation

**Measures of frequency**
- **Incidence** — new cases per population per time (risk of getting it)
- **Prevalence** — existing cases at a point in time (burden)
- **Attack rate** — proportion infected in a defined outbreak population
- **Case fatality rate** vs **infection fatality rate** — denominator matters enormously

**Measures of transmission**
- **R₀** — average secondary cases in a fully susceptible population
- **Rt** — the same, given current immunity and behaviour; the number that guides action
- **Serial interval** and **incubation period** — set the pace of an outbreak

**Study designs**, weakest to strongest for causality: case report → cross-sectional → case–control → cohort → randomized trial. Case–control is the workhorse of foodborne outbreak investigation.

**Outbreak investigation steps:** confirm the diagnosis → confirm an outbreak exists → define a case → find cases → describe by person/place/time → generate hypotheses → test them → control measures → communicate. Genomics now enters at the "test hypotheses" step ([[Phylogenomics and Outbreak Typing]]).

**Bias to watch:** selection bias, information bias, confounding, and — in genomic epidemiology — sampling bias, which is usually the dominant problem.

## Clinical Importance
- Pre-test probability comes from local epidemiology; the same symptoms mean different things in different settings
- Empirical therapy depends on local resistance prevalence

## Research Importance
- Quantifying intervention effect; linking exposure to outcome

## Diagnostic Relevance
- Sensitivity/specificity are properties of a test; PPV/NPV depend on prevalence — see [[Model Evaluation in Clinical Microbiology]]

## AMR Relevance
- Surveillance networks quantify resistance trends and guide empirical guidelines
