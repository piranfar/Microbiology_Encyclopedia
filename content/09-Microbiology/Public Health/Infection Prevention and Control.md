# Infection Prevention and Control

## One-Sentence Definition

Infection prevention and control (IPC) is the set of practices that stop pathogens moving between patients, staff, and the healthcare environment.

## Simple Explanation

Break the chain of transmission — most of it is hand hygiene, and most of the failure is hand hygiene.

## Detailed Scientific Explanation

**Chain of infection:** reservoir → portal of exit → mode of transmission → portal of entry → susceptible host. IPC breaks any link.

**Standard precautions** apply to every patient: hand hygiene, PPE by anticipated exposure, safe injection practice, sharps safety, environmental cleaning, and respiratory etiquette.

**Transmission-based precautions**

| Type | Used for | Key measure |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Contact | MRSA, VRE, CRE, *[[Clostridioides difficile]]* | Gloves + gown; *C. difficile* needs soap and water and a sporicidal agent — alcohol gel does not kill spores |
| Droplet | Influenza, meningococcus, pertussis | Surgical mask, ~1–2 m distance |
| Airborne | TB, measles, varicella | N95/FFP, negative-pressure room |

**Device bundles** are the highest-yield interventions: central line insertion and maintenance bundles ([[CLABSI]]), catheter-associated UTI bundles, ventilator bundles, and surgical site infection prevention (timing of prophylaxis, normothermia, glucose control).

**Outbreak response in hospitals:** case definition, screening of contacts, cohorting, environmental sampling when justified, and genomic confirmation of clusters ([[MLST and cgMLST]]).

**Why hand hygiene still fails:** compliance is measured at ~40–60% in many settings without sustained intervention. [[Ignaz Semmelweis]] demonstrated the effect in 1847 and was rejected.

## Clinical Importance
- Directly prevents healthcare-associated infections, which are a major source of resistant-organism exposure

## Research Importance
- Implementation science; behaviour change; environmental microbiology of hospitals

## AMR Relevance
Together with [[Antimicrobial Stewardship]] and [[Vaccination]], IPC is one of the three practical levers against [[Antimicrobial Resistance]] — and the only one that stops a resistant organism spreading once it exists.
