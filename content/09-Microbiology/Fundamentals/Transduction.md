# Transduction

## One-Sentence Definition

Transduction is phage-mediated transfer of bacterial DNA from one bacterium to another.

## Simple Explanation

A virus that infects bacteria accidentally (or specifically) ships bacterial genes to a new host.

## Detailed Scientific Explanation

- **Generalized transduction:** lytic phage packages random bacterial DNA fragments (e.g., classic P1 in *E. coli*).
- **Specialized transduction:** temperate phage excises incorrectly and carries flanking bacterial genes (e.g., λ).
- Lysogeny can also bring phage-encoded toxins (phage conversion): diphtheria toxin, cholera toxin, Shiga-like toxins, some staphylococcal toxins.

## Mechanism

Phage infection → packaging of bacterial DNA or prophage excision errors → infection of recipient → recombination or lysogeny → new traits.

## Clinical Importance

- Toxinogenic conversion creates major pathogens from otherwise milder strains.
- Contributes to accessory genome diversity.

## Research Importance

- Classic genetic mapping tool; modern synthetic biology still uses phage-derived parts.

## Diagnostic Relevance

- PCR for toxin genes may detect phage-borne virulence factors.

## AMR Relevance

- Can move resistance genes in some systems; generally secondary to plasmids/conjugation for MDR hospital epidemics, but relevant in genomic studies.

## Related Organisms

- *Corynebacterium diphtheriae*, *Vibrio cholerae*, STEC, *S. aureus* (toxin phages)
