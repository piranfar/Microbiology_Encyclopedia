# Prokaryote vs Eukaryote

## One-Sentence Definition

Prokaryotes are cells without a membrane-bound nucleus (Bacteria, Archaea); eukaryotes have a true nucleus and organelles (fungi, parasites, human cells).

## Simple Explanation

Bacteria are “simpler” factory cells: DNA free in the cytoplasm, no mitochondria. Human cells and fungi package DNA in a nucleus and have specialized organelles.

## Detailed Scientific Explanation

| Feature | Prokaryotes | Eukaryotes |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Nucleus | No (nucleoid) | Yes |
| Organelles | Absent / limited | Mitochondria, ER, etc. |
| Size | Typically 0.2–5 µm | Typically 10–100 µm |
| Cell wall | Peptidoglycan (Bacteria) or other (Archaea) | Fungi: chitin; animals: none |
| Ribosomes | 70S (30S+50S) | 80S (40S+60S); mitochondria 70S |
| Division | Binary fission | Mitosis / meiosis |
| Examples | *E. coli*, *S. aureus* | *Candida*, *Plasmodium*, humans |

Viruses are acellular and fit neither category.

## Mechanism

Compartmentalization in eukaryotes separates transcription/translation and enables complex signaling. Prokaryotes couple transcription–translation and grow faster with smaller genomes.

## Clinical Importance

- Antibiotics often exploit prokaryote-specific targets (cell wall, 70S ribosome).
- Fungi/parasites need different drugs because they are eukaryotic.

## Research Importance

- Domains of life ([[Carl Woese]]): Bacteria, Archaea, Eukarya.
- Horizontal gene transfer is far more central in prokaryotes.

## Diagnostic Relevance

- Morphology and staining assume prokaryotic wall chemistry ([[Gram Stain]]).
- Size/shape under [[light microscope]] guides first classification.

## AMR Relevance

- Resistance evolves rapidly in prokaryotes via mutation + [[Horizontal Gene Transfer]].
