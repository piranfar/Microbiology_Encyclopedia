# Pathogen

## One-Sentence Definition

A pathogen is a microorganism (or infectious agent) capable of causing disease in a host.

## Simple Explanation

A microbe that can make you sick — not just live quietly on or in you.

## Detailed Scientific Explanation

- **Frank (primary) pathogens:** cause disease in healthy hosts (e.g., *M. tuberculosis*, influenza).
- **Opportunistic pathogens:** cause disease mainly when barriers/immunity fail (e.g., *Candida*, *P. aeruginosa* in compromised hosts).
- Pathogenicity = ability to cause disease; virulence = degree of damage / severity.
- Depends on host factors: dose, route, immune status, microbiome context.
- Contrast with [[Normal Microbiota]] (usually commensal/mutualist; can become opportunistic).

## Mechanism

Virulence factors: adhesion, invasion, toxins, immune evasion ([[Capsule]], antigenic variation), nutrient acquisition. Outcome = pathogen strategies × host response ([[MOC - Immunology]]).

## Clinical Importance

- Frames every infectious disease differential.
- Guides isolation precautions and empiric therapy by likely pathogen class.

## Research Importance

- Molecular Koch’s postulates test candidate virulence genes ([[Koch’s Postulates]]).

## Diagnostic Relevance

- Goal of lab workup: detect/identify pathogens (or rule them out) in clinical context — not every detected organism is the cause ([[PCR]]).

## AMR Relevance

- Pathogens under drug pressure evolve resistance; colonizers can be reservoirs of resistance genes.
