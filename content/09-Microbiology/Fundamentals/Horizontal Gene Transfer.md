# Horizontal Gene Transfer

## One-Sentence Definition

Horizontal gene transfer (HGT) is the movement of genetic material between organisms other than by parent-to-offspring inheritance — the main engine of rapid bacterial evolution and AMR spread.

## Simple Explanation

Bacteria can swap useful DNA with neighbors (or pick it up from the environment), not only pass genes to their daughters.

## Detailed Scientific Explanation

Three classical routes:

| Route | Vehicle | Note |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| [[Transformation]] | Free DNA | Competence systems |
| [[Transduction]] | Bacteriophage | Generalized / specialized |
| [[Conjugation]] | Direct cell contact | Plasmids, ICEs |

Mobile genetic elements: [[Plasmid]]s, [[Transposons and Insertion Sequences]], [[Integrons]], [[Integrative Conjugative Elements]], [[Genomic Islands]]. Defense systems ([[CRISPR-Cas in Bacteria]], [[Restriction-Modification Systems]]) modulate which transfers succeed. HGT blurs species boundaries for traits like resistance and virulence.

## Mechanism

Donor DNA enters a recipient → recombination or autonomous replication (plasmids) → selectable phenotype (e.g., antibiotic survival) enriches successful transfers.

## Clinical Importance

- Explains sudden appearance of multidrug-resistant clones and virulence packages.
- Infection control must consider plasmid epidemiology, not only clonal spread.

## Research Importance

- Core of microbial genomics and One Health AMR surveillance.
- Molecular Koch frameworks often involve transferring candidate virulence genes.

## Diagnostic Relevance

- WGS and PCR panels detect resistance/virulence genes that may be mobile.
- Gene presence ≠ expression, but HGT explains unexpected resistance profiles.

## AMR Relevance

**Central.** Most clinically critical resistance (ESBLs, carbapenemases, *vanA*, many *mcr* genes) rides on mobile elements via HGT.
