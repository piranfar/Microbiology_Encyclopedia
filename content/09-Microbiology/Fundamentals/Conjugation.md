# Conjugation

## One-Sentence Definition

Conjugation is direct, contact-dependent DNA transfer between bacteria, usually of a [[Plasmid]] (or integrative conjugative element), via a conjugative pilus/type IV secretion machinery.

## Simple Explanation

Two bacteria connect and one sends a copy of a plasmid to the other — like sharing a resistance USB stick.

## Detailed Scientific Explanation

- F plasmid in *E. coli* is the textbook model (tra genes, pilus).
- Many clinical resistance plasmids are conjugative or mobilizable.
- Transfer is typically ssDNA with rolling-circle replication; recipient becomes a new donor if tra genes are complete.
- Can occur across species/genera, especially among Enterobacterales and other Gram-negatives.

## Mechanism

Donor–recipient contact → mating channel → plasmid transfer → complementary strand synthesis → heritable plasmid in recipient.

## Clinical Importance

- Primary route for epidemic ESBL/carbapenemase plasmid spread in hospitals and One Health reservoirs.
- Explains identical resistance genes in unrelated species.

## Research Importance

- Plasmid typing (Inc groups), conjugation frequency assays, plasmid epidemiology.

## Diagnostic Relevance

- AST shows phenotype; WGS/PCR show mobile gene context (plasmid vs chromosome).

## AMR Relevance

**Highest-yield HGT route for clinical MDR.** Infection control and stewardship respond to clonal *and* plasmid outbreaks.
