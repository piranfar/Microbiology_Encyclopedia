# Transformation

## One-Sentence Definition

Transformation is uptake of naked DNA from the environment by a competent bacterial cell and its heritable incorporation.

## Simple Explanation

A bacterium swallows loose DNA lying around — sometimes gaining new traits.

## Detailed Scientific Explanation

- Natural competence: *Streptococcus pneumoniae*, *Bacillus subtilis*, *Haemophilus*, *Neisseria*, etc.
- Artificial competence: lab methods (CaCl₂, electroporation) for cloning.
- Griffith’s experiment (rough/smooth pneumococci) historically revealed transformation.
- DNA must escape restriction and usually integrate by homologous recombination (chromosomal) or establish if plasmid-capable.

## Mechanism

Competence machinery binds DNA → transport across envelope → recombination or plasmid establishment → phenotype if expressed.

## Clinical Importance

- Capsule locus swapping / serotype changes in pneumococci.
- Possible contribution to mosaic PBPs and penicillin non-susceptibility.

## Research Importance

- Foundational for molecular cloning and reverse genetics.

## Diagnostic Relevance

- Indirect: explains genetic plasticity seen in WGS of competent pathogens.

## AMR Relevance

- Can disseminate resistance alleles when free DNA is available (less dominant clinically than conjugation for many MDR plasmids, but important in some species).

## Related Organisms

- *S. pneumoniae*, *Neisseria* spp., *H. influenzae*
