# Biofilm

## One-Sentence Definition

A biofilm is a surface-attached microbial community embedded in a self-produced extracellular matrix, showing altered growth and high tolerance to antimicrobials and immune clearance.

## Simple Explanation

Bacteria build sticky cities on surfaces (catheters, valves, teeth) where drugs and immune cells struggle to reach them.

## Detailed Scientific Explanation

- Stages: attachment → microcolonies → mature matrix → dispersal.
- Matrix: polysaccharides, proteins, eDNA.
- Quorum sensing coordinates density-dependent behaviors.
- Classic clinical: device infections, endocarditis vegetations, *P. aeruginosa* in CF airways, dental plaque.

## Mechanism

Slow growth + matrix diffusion barrier + persister cells → antibiotic *tolerance* (often without new resistance genes). Distinct from genetic [[Mutation and Selection]] resistance, though both can coexist.

## Clinical Importance

- Device removal often required for cure.
- Longer / combination regimens; relapse common if biofilm persists.

## Diagnostic Relevance

- Standard AST on planktonic cells underestimates biofilm tolerance.
- Sonication of explanted devices improves culture yield.

## AMR Relevance

- Biofilms are reservoirs for HGT and chronic selection under antibiotics.
