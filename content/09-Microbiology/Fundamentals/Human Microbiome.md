# Human Microbiome

## One-Sentence Definition

The human microbiome is the community of bacteria, archaea, fungi, and viruses that inhabit the body surfaces and cavities, together with their genes and metabolic activities.

## Simple Explanation

Most microbes on and in you are not trying to cause disease — they are part of how your body works.

## Detailed Scientific Explanation

**Site-specific communities.** Gut, skin, oral cavity, airway, and vagina each have distinct, characteristic communities. Diversity is generally protective in the gut; the healthy vagina is the opposite, dominated by *Lactobacillus* with low diversity.

**Functions**
- **Colonization resistance** — occupying niches, consuming nutrients, producing bacteriocins and secondary bile acids that suppress pathogens
- **Metabolism** — short-chain fatty acids from fibre fermentation; vitamin K and B synthesis; drug metabolism
- **Immune education** — development of regulatory T cells, IgA induction, mucosal barrier maintenance

**Perturbation.** Antibiotics cause rapid loss of diversity with incomplete recovery. The clearest clinical consequence is *[[Clostridioides difficile]]* infection following loss of colonization resistance, and faecal microbiota transplantation restores it — one of the few microbiome interventions with strong randomized evidence.

**Interpretation caution.** Most microbiome–disease associations are correlational, confounded by diet, medication, and geography, and inconsistent between studies. Methodological choices dominate the results — see [[Microbiome Statistics]].

## Mechanism
Studied by [[16S Amplicon Analysis]] (who is there) and shotgun [[Metagenomics]] (who is there and what they can do), with [[Metagenome-Assembled Genomes]] reconstructing uncultured members.

## Clinical Importance
- Distinguishing colonization from infection is a daily diagnostic problem ([[Normal Microbiota]])
- Microbiome damage is an under-counted harm of antibiotic overuse ([[Antimicrobial Stewardship]])

## Research Importance
- Live biotherapeutics, microbiome-based diagnostics, drug–microbiome interactions

## AMR Relevance
The gut is a major reservoir of resistance genes — the **resistome** — and a mixing vessel for [[Horizontal Gene Transfer]].
