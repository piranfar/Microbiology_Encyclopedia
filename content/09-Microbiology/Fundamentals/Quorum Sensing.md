# Quorum Sensing

## One-Sentence Definition

Quorum sensing (QS) is density-dependent cell-to-cell communication in which bacteria produce, release, and detect autoinducer signals to coordinate group behaviors such as virulence, competence, and biofilm maturation.

## Simple Explanation

Bacteria vote with chemistry: when enough neighbors are around, the signal level crosses a threshold and the whole crowd switches on shared programs (attack, stick together, share DNA).

## Detailed Scientific Explanation

| System | Signal class | Typical organisms |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| LuxI/LuxR | Acyl-homoserine lactones (AHL) | Many Gram-negatives |
| Agr | Autoinducing peptides (AIP) | *Staphylococcus* |
| Com / CSP | Competence-stimulating peptides | Streptococci |
| AI-2 (LuxS) | Shared interspecies signal | Broad |
| PQS / quinolones | *Pseudomonas* specialty | *P. aeruginosa* |

*P. aeruginosa* stacks LasI/R, RhlI/R, and PQS hierarchies controlling toxins, proteases, and biofilm. *S. aureus* Agr controls toxin vs adhesin expression in a growth-phase switch.

QS inhibitors and quenchers (lactonases, competitive analogs) are experimental antivirulence strategies.

## Mechanism

Synthase produces autoinducer → extracellular accumulation with density → receptor/transcription factor activation → regulon expression (virulence, exoenzymes, competence, sporulation cues).

## Clinical Importance

- Explains why some toxins appear late in infection or in abscesses/biofilms.
- Device-related infections ([[Biofilm]]) are QS-shaped communities, not planktonic monocultures.

## Research Importance

- Target for antivirulence drugs that reduce selection for classical resistance.
- Synthetic biology uses QS circuits as genetic switches.

## Diagnostic Relevance

- Indirect: phenotype timing and transcriptomics ([[Microbial Transcriptomics]]); not routine clinical assays yet.

## AMR Relevance

Indirect but important: biofilms and persister physiology under QS control raise antibiotic tolerance ([[Persisters and Antibiotic Tolerance]]); QS inhibition may potentiate antibiotics.

## Learning Aids

### Clinical Example
> [!example]
> **Case:** Ventilator-associated pneumonia with *P. aeruginosa*; isolates show strong elastase/pyocyanin in vitro at high density.
> **Question:** Which regulatory theme links density to these toxins?
> **Answer:** Quorum sensing (Las/Rhl/PQS cascade) coordinating secreted virulence factors.
