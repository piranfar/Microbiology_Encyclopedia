# Plasmid

## One-Sentence Definition

A plasmid is an extrachromosomal DNA molecule (usually circular) that replicates autonomously and often carries accessory genes such as antibiotic resistance or virulence factors.

## Simple Explanation

Optional bonus DNA — not required for basic life, but can make a bacterium harder to kill or more dangerous.

## Detailed Scientific Explanation

- Copy number: low vs high.
- Incompatibility (Inc) groups: plasmids of same Inc can’t stably coexist.
- Conjugative plasmids encode transfer machinery; mobilizable plasmids hitchhike.
- R-plasmids: resistance; virulence plasmids (e.g., some *Bacillus anthracis*, *Shigella*/*E. coli* systems).
- Can integrate transiently or evolve into chromosomally integrated forms; related: ICEs, integrative elements.

## Mechanism

Independent replication origins; segregation systems; toxin–antitoxin modules promote persistence. Transfer mainly via [[Conjugation]].

## Clinical Importance

- Engine of MDR in Enterobacterales and beyond.
- Loss of plasmid in vitro can make AST look “susceptible” while clinical strains retain it — interpret carefully.

## Research Importance

- Cloning vectors are engineered plasmids.
- Plasmidomics is a major AMR research field.

## Diagnostic Relevance

- PCR/WGS detect plasmid-borne genes (*bla*KPC, *bla*NDM, *mcr-1*, etc.).
- Phenotype via [[Antimicrobial Susceptibility Testing]].

## AMR Relevance

- Primary storage unit for many clinically critical resistance genes.
- Co-selection: one plasmid, many resistance cassettes.
