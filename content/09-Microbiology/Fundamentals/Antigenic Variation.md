# Antigenic Variation

## One-Sentence Definition

Antigenic variation is the ability of a pathogen to change its surface antigens so that existing host immunity no longer recognizes it.

## Simple Explanation

The pathogen changes its face so the immune system's memory no longer matches.

## Detailed Scientific Explanation

**Genetic strategies**

| Strategy | How | Example |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Point mutation (drift)** | Error-prone replication accumulates changes in epitopes | Influenza haemagglutinin antigenic drift; HIV envelope |
| **Reassortment / recombination (shift)** | Segmented genomes swap segments | Influenza A pandemic strains |
| **Gene conversion** | Silent cassettes copied into an expression site | *Trypanosoma brucei* VSG; *Neisseria gonorrhoeae* pilin |
| **Phase variation** | Reversible on/off switching of expression | *Salmonella* flagellar phase variation; *Neisseria* Opa |
| **Serotype diversity** | Many stable capsule types in the population | *[[Streptococcus pneumoniae]]* (>90 capsule serotypes) |

**Consequences**
- **Reinfection** with the "same" pathogen throughout life
- **Vaccine escape and reformulation** — seasonal influenza vaccine strain selection each year
- **Serotype replacement** after conjugate vaccine introduction: non-vaccine pneumococcal serotypes expand into the vacated niche
- Chronic infection despite an active immune response (trypanosomiasis, HIV)

## Mechanism
Selection acts on pre-existing variation generated by mutation, recombination, or programmed genetic switching; the host's antibody response is the selective force.

## Clinical Importance
- Explains why some vaccines need annual updating and why others give lifelong protection
- Explains failure of serology and of some antigen-detection tests against divergent strains
