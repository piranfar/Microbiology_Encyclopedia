# Capsule

## One-Sentence Definition

A capsule is a organized polysaccharide (sometimes protein) layer outside the cell wall that protects bacteria from phagocytosis and desiccation and can be a vaccine antigen.

## Simple Explanation

A slimy coat that helps bacteria hide from immune cells and stick around in the host.

## Detailed Scientific Explanation

- Usually polysaccharide; well-defined capsules vs looser slime layers.
- Classic encapsulated pathogens: *Streptococcus pneumoniae*, *Haemophilus influenzae* type b, *Neisseria meningitidis*, *Klebsiella pneumoniae*, *Bacillus anthracis* (poly-D-glutamate protein capsule).
- Quellung reaction historically detected pneumococcal capsules.
- Biofilms involve extracellular polymeric substance related to, but not identical with, classic capsules.

## Mechanism

Inhibits opsonophagocytosis; masks surface antigens; may aid adherence. Anticapsular antibody → protection (basis of polysaccharide/conjugate vaccines).

## Clinical Importance

- Major virulence factor for meningitis, pneumonia, bacteremia pathogens.
- Capsule-based vaccines (PCV, Hib, MenACWY/MenB strategies).

## Research Importance

- Capsule switching and serotype replacement after vaccination.
- Capsule genetics as virulence and typing markers.

## Diagnostic Relevance

- India ink / negative stain for *Cryptococcus* (fungal capsule).
- Mucoid colony morphology (*Klebsiella*) suggests heavy capsule.

## AMR Relevance

- Indirect: successful colonization/infection increases antibiotic exposure of populations; not a primary resistance mechanism.

## Related Organisms

- *S. pneumoniae*, *K. pneumoniae*, *N. meningitidis*, *H. influenzae*, *B. anthracis*
