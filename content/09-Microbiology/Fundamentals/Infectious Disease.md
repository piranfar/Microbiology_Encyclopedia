# Infectious Disease

## One-Sentence Definition

An infectious disease is a clinically evident illness caused by a [[Pathogen]] (or its toxin) invading or intoxicating a host, often with potential for transmission.

## Simple Explanation

When a microbe (or its poison) makes you sick — sometimes contagious, sometimes not.

## Detailed Scientific Explanation

Chain of infection: reservoir → portal of exit → transmission → portal of entry → susceptible host. Outcomes range from asymptomatic colonization to severe systemic disease. Distinctions:
- Infection vs colonization vs contamination
- Communicable vs non-communicable (e.g., tetanus from spores in soil)
- Local vs systemic; acute vs chronic; latent (e.g., herpesviruses, TB)

Rooted in [[Germ Theory]]; proven historically via [[Koch’s Postulates]].

## Mechanism

Pathogen virulence × inoculum × host immunity × microbiome × environment. Damage from invasion, toxins, or immune pathology ([[Pathology]]).

## Clinical Importance

- Organizes syndromes by site → [[MOC - Clinical Microbiology]]
- Drives isolation, vaccines, antimicrobials, public health reporting.

## Research Importance

- Epidemiology, pathogenesis models, vaccine/AMR research.

## Diagnostic Relevance

- Syndromic workup: choose specimen + method ([[MOC - Diagnostic & Lab Methods]]).
- Gold standards vary by disease (culture, serology, NAAT, microscopy).

## AMR Relevance

- Treatment failures and prolonged shedding amplify resistance selection and spread.
