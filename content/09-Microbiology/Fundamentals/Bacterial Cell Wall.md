# Bacterial Cell Wall

## One-Sentence Definition

The bacterial cell wall is a rigid, protective layer outside the cytoplasmic membrane, built mainly of peptidoglycan, that determines shape and Gram-stain behavior.

## Simple Explanation

Bacteria wear armor. Gram-positives have thick armor (retain purple); Gram-negatives have thin armor plus an outer membrane (stain pink).

## Detailed Scientific Explanation

**Peptidoglycan (murein):** alternating N-acetylglucosamine (NAG) and N-acetylmuramic acid (NAM) chains cross-linked by peptides.

**Gram-positive:** thick peptidoglycan; teichoic / lipoteichoic acids; no outer membrane.

**Gram-negative:** thin peptidoglycan in the periplasm; outer membrane with LPS (endotoxin); porins for nutrient entry.

**Exceptions / variants:**
- *Mycobacterium*: mycolic acids (acid-fast) — see [[Acid-Fast Stain]]
- *Mycoplasma*: no cell wall
- *Chlamydia*: atypical wall / limited peptidoglycan

## Mechanism

Wall resists osmotic lysis. β-lactams block cross-linking (PBPs); vancomycin blocks peptide substrate; lysozyme cleaves glycan bonds.

## Clinical Importance

- Major antibiotic target class.
- LPS (Gram-neg) drives septic shock pathophysiology.
- Wall structure predicts empiric therapy after [[Gram Stain]].

## Research Importance

- Peptidoglycan synthesis pathway = drug discovery target.
- Outer-membrane permeability barrier explains many Gram-neg resistance phenotypes.

## Diagnostic Relevance

- Basis of [[Gram Stain]].
- Wall-less / atypical organisms need special stains or molecular methods.

## AMR Relevance

- β-lactamases, PBP alteration, porin loss, efflux — all interact with wall/envelope biology.
- Vancomycin resistance alters wall peptide termini (*van* genes).

## Related Organisms

- Gram+: *Staphylococcus*, *Streptococcus*, *Clostridium*
- Gram−: *E. coli*, *Pseudomonas*, *Neisseria*
- Acid-fast: *Mycobacterium*
