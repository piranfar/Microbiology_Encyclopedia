# Shigella spp.

## One-Sentence Definition

*Shigella* species are non-motile, lactose-non-fermenting Enterobacterales that cause bacillary dysentery via intestinal invasion and Shiga toxin (some species) — low infectious dose and person-to-person spread.

## Taxonomy & Morphology
- *S. dysenteriae*, *S. flexneri*, *S. boydii*, *S. sonnei*
- Gram-negative bacillus; non-lactose on MacConkey; closely related to [[Escherichia coli]] phylogenetically

## Virulence Highlights
- M cell invasion; intercellular spread
- Shiga toxin (*S. dysenteriae* type 1) — HUS risk (similar mechanism to STEC)
- Very low inoculum — outbreaks in crowded settings

## Clinical Syndromes
- Bloody diarrhea, cramps, fever (dysentery)
- Complications: dehydration, HUS (toxin-mediated), reactive arthritis

## Diagnosis
- Stool culture on selective media; [[PCR]] on GI panels
- Distinguish from STEC — toxin testing and epidemiology

## Treatment Notes (conceptual)
- Supportive hydration central
- Antibiotics shorten shedding in severe cases — resistance common; follow local guidelines
- Avoid antimotility agents in invasive diarrhea when possible

## AMR Relevance
- MDR *Shigella* globally (fluoroquinolone, azithromycin resistance patterns vary)
