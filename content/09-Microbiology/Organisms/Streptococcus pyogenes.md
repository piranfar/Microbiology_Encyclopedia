# Streptococcus pyogenes

## One-Sentence Definition

*Streptococcus pyogenes* (Group A Streptococcus) is a Gram-positive coccus in chains causing pharyngitis, SSTI, necrotizing fasciitis, toxin-mediated shock, and post-infectious sequelae (RF, PSGN).

## Taxonomy & Morphology
- β-hemolytic; Lancefield group A
- Catalase-negative; bacitracin-sensitive (classic teaching)
- [[Gram Stain]]: chains / pairs

## Virulence Highlights
- M protein (antiphagocytic; emm typing)
- Streptolysins, hyaluronidase, streptokinase
- Superantigens (scarlet fever / STSS)
- [[Capsule]] (hyaluronic acid) in some strains

## Clinical Syndromes
- Pharyngitis, impetigo, erysipelas, cellulitis
- Necrotizing fasciitis, streptococcal toxic shock
- Rheumatic fever; post-strep glomerulonephritis

## Diagnosis
- Throat culture / RADT for pharyngitis
- Wound/blood culture for invasive disease
- ASO/anti-DNase B for post-strep syndromes (serology)

## Treatment Notes (conceptual)
- Penicillin remains drug of choice when susceptible (almost universal for GAS)
- Add clindamycin in toxin-severe invasive disease (toxin suppression adjunct — clinical practice point)
- Surgical source control for necrotizing infection

## AMR
- Penicillin resistance not a practical problem in GAS; macrolide resistance varies by region
