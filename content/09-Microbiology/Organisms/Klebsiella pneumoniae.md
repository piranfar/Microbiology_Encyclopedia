# Klebsiella pneumoniae

## One-Sentence Definition

*Klebsiella pneumoniae* is a Gram-negative, usually heavily encapsulated Enterobacterales rod causing pneumonia, UTI, and invasive disease — notorious for carbapenemase-producing MDR clones.

## Taxonomy & Morphology
- Gram-negative bacillus; lactose fermenter; non-motile
- Mucoid colonies from prominent [[Capsule]]
- [[Gram Stain]]: pink rods ± capsule halo impression

## Virulence Highlights
- Capsule (hypervirulent strains: liver abscess, metastatic spread)
- Siderophores; LPS
- Hospital adaptation + plasmid AMR

## Clinical Syndromes
- HAP/VAP, UTI, bacteremia, wound infection
- Hypervirulent pathotype: community liver abscess (esp. in Asia; increasingly global)

## Diagnosis
- Culture + ID (MALDI/biochemicals)
- AST critical; molecular carbapenemase detection where available
- WGS for outbreak tracing

## Treatment Notes (conceptual)
- Directed by AST; carbapenem-resistant isolates need specialist regimens (ceftazidime-avibactam, etc. — region-specific)
- Source control for abscesses

## AMR
- ESBL common; CRE with KPC, NDM, OXA-48-like enzymes — typically [[Plasmid]] / [[Conjugation]]
- Core organism for [[MOC - Antimicrobial Resistance (AMR)]] and [[Paper - AMR Database M.Centner 2026]]
