# Hepatitis C Virus

## One-Sentence Definition

Hepatitis C virus (HCV) is a positive-sense RNA flavivirus causing often silent chronic hepatitis cured in most cases with direct-acting antivirals (DAAs), with parenteral transmission historically dominant.

## Taxonomy & Morphology
- Flaviviridae; (+)ssRNA; envelope
- High genetic diversity (genotypes 1–6) — affects DAA choice historically

## Virulence Highlights
- Chronic infection common without treatment
- Oncogenic risk (HCC with cirrhosis)
- Extrahepatic manifestations (cryoglobulinemia, etc.)

## Clinical Syndromes
- Acute hepatitis (often mild/asymptomatic)
- Chronic hepatitis → cirrhosis, HCC
- Reinfection possible after cure

## Diagnosis
| Step | Test | Note |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Screen | anti-HCV antibody | Past or current infection |
| Confirm | HCV RNA [[PCR]] | Active viremia |
| Monitor | RNA quant + genotype/subtype | Treatment response |

## Treatment / Prevention
- **DAAs** — >95% cure rates in many genotypes
- No vaccine (antigenic variation, lack of durable protective immunity model)
- Harm reduction; blood supply screening
