# Varicella-Zoster Virus

## One-Sentence Definition

Varicella-zoster virus (VZV) is a neurotropic alpha-herpesvirus causing primary varicella (chickenpox) and reactivation zoster (shingles), preventable by live-attenuated or recombinant subunit vaccines.

## Taxonomy & Morphology
- Human herpesvirus 3; dsDNA; envelope; latent in sensory ganglia
- Related: [[HSV]] — similar latency/reactivation biology

## Virulence Highlights
- Respiratory spread (varicella) → viremia → vesicular rash
- Latency in dorsal root ganglia → zoster with dermatomal rash
- Immunocompromised: disseminated disease, visceral involvement

## Clinical Syndromes
- Primary varicella (often childhood)
- Herpes zoster — painful dermatomal vesicles; postherpetic neuralgia
- Disseminated zoster in immunosuppression
- CNS: meningitis/encephalitis (less common than [[HSV Encephalitis]])

## Diagnosis
- Clinical often sufficient for typical zoster
- [[PCR]] on vesicle fluid/CSF when atypical or severe
- Serology: IgG immunity vs acute (context-dependent)

## Treatment / Prevention
- Antivirals (acyclovir/valacyclovir) for zoster/complicated varicella
- Vaccines: childhood varicella; shingles vaccine (recombinant preferred in older adults)
- Isolation for susceptible contacts (airborne + contact)

## Genomics & Surveillance
- Clade tracking in research; vaccine strain vs wild-type differentiation occasionally relevant
