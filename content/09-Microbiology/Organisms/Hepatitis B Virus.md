# Hepatitis B Virus

## One-Sentence Definition

Hepatitis B virus (HBV) is a partially double-stranded DNA hepadnavirus causing acute and chronic hepatitis, cirrhosis, and hepatocellular carcinoma — preventable by highly effective subunit vaccination.

## Taxonomy & Morphology
- Hepadnaviridae; small enveloped DNA virus
- Replicates via RNA intermediate (reverse transcriptase) — not a retrovirus

## Virulence Highlights
- cccDNA persistence in nucleus → chronicity
- HBsAg, anti-HBc, anti-HBs serologic patterns define infection phase
- High chronicity risk if acquired perinatally

## Clinical Syndromes
- Acute hepatitis (± fulminant)
- Chronic hepatitis → cirrhosis, HCC
- Occult HBV (low-level DNA, complex serology)

## Diagnosis
| Marker | Meaning |
| :--- | :--- |
| HBsAg | Active infection |
| anti-HBc IgM | Acute/recent |
| anti-HBs | Immunity (vaccine or recovery) |
| HBV DNA [[PCR]] | Viremia; treatment monitoring |

## Treatment / Prevention
- **Vaccine** (HBsAg) — neonatal schedule cornerstone ([[Vaccination]])
- PEP: HBIG + vaccine after exposure
- Chronic: antivirals (tenofovir, entecavir) — long-term suppression
