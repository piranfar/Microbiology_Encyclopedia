# Measles Virus

## One-Sentence Definition

Measles virus is a highly contagious morbillivirus causing fever, cough, coryza, conjunctivitis, and koplik spots followed by maculopapular rash — preventable by live vaccine but dangerous in under-vaccinated populations (SSPE, pneumonia, encephalitis).

## Taxonomy & Morphology
- Paramyxoviridae; (−)ssRNA; envelope; single serotype (genotypes for surveillance)

## Virulence Highlights
- Transmitted via respiratory aerosols — extreme contagion (high R₀)
- Immune amnesia — erases B-cell repertoire (research/clinical significance)
- Rare late complication: SSPE (years after infection)

## Clinical Syndromes
- Classic measles rash illness
- Complications: otitis, pneumonia (primary or bacterial superinfection), encephalitis
- Immunocompromised: giant cell pneumonia

## Diagnosis
- Clinical in outbreak context
- [[Serology in Clinical Microbiology]]: IgM acute; IgG immunity
- [[PCR]] on urine/blood/throat — public health confirmation

## Treatment / Prevention
- Supportive care; ribavirin in select severe cases (specialist)
- **MMR vaccine** — two-dose schedule; herd immunity needs very high coverage ([[Vaccination]])
- Post-exposure prophylaxis: vaccine or IG in window for susceptible contacts
