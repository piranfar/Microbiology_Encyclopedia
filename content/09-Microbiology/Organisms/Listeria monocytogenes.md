# Listeria monocytogenes

## One-Sentence Definition

*Listeria monocytogenes* is a Gram-positive rod that survives refrigeration and invades host cells, causing foodborne gastroenteritis and life-threatening meningitis/bacteremia in pregnant, elderly, and immunocompromised patients.

## Taxonomy & Morphology
- Gram-positive short rods; tumbling motility at room temperature
- Facultative intracellular pathogen; grows at 4 °C (cold enrichment)
- Catalase-positive; β-hemolytic (narrow)

## Virulence Highlights
- Internalins (InlA/InlB) — epithelial/placental invasion
- Listeriolysin O (LLO) — escape from phagosome
- ActA — actin-based intracellular motility (cell-to-cell spread)
- Stress survival in food chain (biofilm on equipment)

## Clinical Syndromes
- Febrile gastroenteritis (outbreaks)
- Bacteremia, meningoencephalitis (rhombencephalitis)
- Pregnancy: amnionitis, stillbirth, neonatal sepsis (early/late)

## Diagnosis
- Blood/CSF culture; cold enrichment for food
- MALDI / biochemicals; PCR panels increasingly include *Listeria*
- Notify public health for invasive cases

## Treatment Notes (conceptual)
- Ampicillin ± gentamicin for synergy in invasive disease (classic regimen logic)
- TMP-SMX alternative in penicillin allergy (confirm local practice)
- Duration longer for meningitis/endocarditis

## AMR
- Generally susceptible to ampicillin historically; resistance uncommon but monitored
- Intrinsic resistance patterns differ from other Gram-positives — know local antibiograms
