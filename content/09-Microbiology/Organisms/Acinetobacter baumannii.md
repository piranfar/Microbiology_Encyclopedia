# Acinetobacter baumannii

## One-Sentence Definition

*Acinetobacter baumannii* is a non-fermenting Gram-negative coccobacillus that persists in the hospital environment and causes ventilator-associated pneumonia, bloodstream infection, and wound infection with extreme drug resistance.

## Taxonomy & Morphology
- Gram-negative coccobacillus, strictly aerobic, oxidase-negative, non-fermenting
- Often appears as pairs; can be mistaken for *Neisseria* on smear
- Grows on MacConkey as a non-lactose fermenter

## Why it survives in hospitals
- Exceptional **desiccation tolerance** — survives weeks on dry surfaces
- Resistant to many disinfectants; strong [[Biofilm]] formation on plastic
- Colonizes ventilators, catheters, bedrails, and the hands of staff
- Highly efficient at acquiring resistance by [[Horizontal Gene Transfer]] — naturally competent for transformation

## Clinical Syndromes
- **Ventilator-associated pneumonia** — the dominant presentation
- Bloodstream infection ([[CLABSI]]), wound and burn infection, occasionally meningitis after neurosurgery
- Combat and disaster-related wound infections
- Distinguishing colonization from infection in respiratory specimens is genuinely difficult

## Diagnosis
- Culture and MALDI-TOF identification ([[Proteomics and MALDI Bioinformatics]])
- Species-level resolution within the *A. calcoaceticus–baumannii* complex may need molecular or [[Whole-Genome Sequencing]] methods

## Treatment & AMR
- **Carbapenem resistance** is now the norm in many regions, usually via **OXA-23** and related class D carbapenemases, often with insertion sequences driving overexpression
- Additional layers: [[Efflux Pumps]], porin loss, aminoglycoside-modifying enzymes
- Remaining options: sulbactam-durlobactam, cefiderocol, polymyxins, sometimes tigecycline or combinations — several are last-line with real toxicity
- Pan-drug-resistant isolates are reported

## Public Health
- The **A** in [[ESKAPE Pathogens]]; a WHO critical-priority pathogen
- Outbreaks are typically environmental plus clonal; genomic typing separates the two ([[MLST and cgMLST]], [[Plasmid and Mobile Element Analysis]])
- Control depends on environmental cleaning and contact precautions ([[Infection Prevention and Control]])
