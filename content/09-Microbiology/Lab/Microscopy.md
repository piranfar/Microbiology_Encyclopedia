# Microscopy

## One-Sentence Definition

Microscopy is the direct visual examination of specimens at magnifications that reveal microorganisms, and it remains the fastest information a microbiology laboratory can produce.

## Simple Explanation

Look at it. Within minutes you know whether there are bacteria, roughly what kind, and whether the specimen was worth processing.

## Modalities

| Modality | Principle | Microbiology use |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Bright-field** | Absorption of transmitted light; needs staining | [[Gram Stain]], [[Acid-Fast Stain]], routine work — see [[light microscope]] |
| **Dark-field** | Only scattered light reaches the objective | Spirochaetes (*Treponema*, *Leptospira*) |
| **Phase contrast** | Converts refractive index differences into contrast | Live unstained cells, motility |
| **Fluorescence** | Fluorophore excitation | Auramine for mycobacteria, DFA, calcofluor white for fungi |
| **Confocal** | Optical sectioning by pinhole | [[Biofilm]] architecture, 3D imaging |
| **Electron (TEM/SEM)** | Electron beam; nanometre resolution | Virus morphology, surface ultrastructure |
| **Super-resolution / cryo-EM** | Beyond the diffraction limit | Research: molecular machines, virion structure |

Resolution limit of light microscopy is ~0.2 µm — which is why most viruses are invisible by light microscopy and why electron microscopy was required to see them.

## Clinical Importance
- A Gram stain of CSF or a positive blood culture changes empirical therapy within minutes
- **Specimen adequacy**: sputum with many squamous epithelial cells is saliva, not sputum — microscopy rejects it before culture wastes two days
- Malaria diagnosis, ova and parasite examination, and fungal wet preps remain microscopy-dependent

## Research Importance
- Single-cell dynamics, persister imaging, host–pathogen interaction imaging

## Diagnostic Relevance
- Rapid, cheap, and universally available — but insensitive (roughly 10⁴–10⁵ organisms/mL needed for a smear) and operator-dependent

## Computational Layer
Automated digital microscopy and CNN-based reading are the most mature clinical AI application in this field — see [[Digital Microscopy and Image AI]].

## History
[[Antonie van Leeuwenhoek]] built the lenses that first revealed "animalcules" in the 1670s; microscopy is the founding technique of the discipline ([[Medical Microbiology History]]).
