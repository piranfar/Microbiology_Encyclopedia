# Antigen Detection Assays

## One-Sentence Definition

Antigen detection assays identify pathogen or toxin proteins directly in clinical specimens — enabling rapid diagnosis via lateral flow, EIA/ELISA, or immunochromatographic cards without culture.

## Simple Explanation

Find pieces of the bug (or its toxin) in the sample — fast yes/no tests like strep swabs or malaria cards.

## Detailed Scientific Explanation

| Format | Examples | Pros / cons |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Lateral flow** | Strep A, influenza, SARS-CoV-2, crypto, malaria RDT | Minutes; variable sensitivity |
| **EIA/ELISA** | *C. diff* toxins, HBsAg, galactomannan | Batchable; needs lab |
| **Agglutination** | Legionella urine Ag, pneumococcal urine Ag | Single analyte; useful in pneumonia |
| **DIF** | HSV/VZV lesion scraping (historical/contextual) | Expertise-dependent |

**Specimen pairing**
- Respiratory antigens — nasopharyngeal swab quality matters
- Urine Ag — pneumococcus, legionella (pneumonia)
- Stool — rotavirus, adeno, *Giardia* (parasitology overlap)

**Interpretation**
- Positive in high-prevalence setting → likely true
- Negative during low viral load — repeat or molecular test
- Does not provide AST ([[Antimicrobial Susceptibility Testing]])

## Mechanism
Immobilized antibody captures target antigen → visible signal (colored line/enzyme color change).

## Clinical Importance
- ED and clinic triage speed
- Outbreak screening (influenza, COVID)

## Diagnostic Relevance
- Bridge before [[PCR]] / culture; syndromic panels combine multiple antigens/nucleic acids
