# Nested PCR

**Related:** [[PCR]] · [[qPCR]] · [[Broad-Range 16S PCR]]

## 1. Principle
> Nested PCR uses two successive amplifications: an outer primer pair first, then inner primers targeting within that amplicon — boosting sensitivity and specificity at the cost of higher contamination risk.

## 2. Step-by-Step Procedure (conceptual)
1. Round 1 with outer primers (fewer cycles).
2. Transfer diluted product (or use single-tube nested designs) into round 2 with inner primers.
3. Detect second-round product.
4. Extreme contamination control: separate rooms, unidirectional workflow, UNG/dUTP where applicable.

## 3. Interpretation
- Ultra-sensitive — interpret low-level positives cautiously.
- **Pitfalls:** Amplicon carryover false positives; modern labs often replace classical open nested PCR with real-time or closed nested systems.

## 4. Clinical Use Cases
- Historical / specialized detection of low-burden pathogens (some parasites, mycobacteria research assays).
- Semi-nested variants still appear in reference protocols.
- Prefer [[qPCR]] or [[Digital PCR]] when validated alternatives exist.

## 5. Comparison with Other Methods
| Method | Sensitivity | Contamination risk |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Nested PCR | Very high | High if open-tube |
| [[qPCR]] | High | Lower (closed) |
| [[Digital PCR]] | High for rare targets | Moderate |

## 6. Mnemonic / Visual Aid
> **Outer door → Inner door** — two locks, two chances to track dirt inside.
