# Macrolides

## One-Sentence Definition

Macrolides (and related ketolides) inhibit the 50S ribosomal subunit, used for atypical respiratory pathogens, some STI alternatives, and select Gram-positive infections — with rising resistance via erm methylases and efflux.

## Simple Explanation

They block the bacterial ribosome factory — handy for atypicals like *Mycoplasma* and *Legionella*, but resistance is common in streptococci and some STI contexts.

## Detailed Scientific Explanation

| Examples | Typical use |
| :--- | :--- |
| Azithromycin | CAP atypicals, chlamydia, some GI infections |
| Clarithromycin | *H. pylori* regimens ([[Helicobacter pylori]]) |
| Erythromycin | Legacy; GI motility side uses |

**Coverage:** atypicals ([[Mycoplasma pneumoniae]], [[Legionella pneumophila]], [[Chlamydia trachomatis]]), some Gram-positives; **not** reliable for serious *S. aureus* bacteremia alone.

**Resistance:** *erm* ribosomal methylation; mef efflux — affects streptococci, staphylococci, GAS treatment failures.

## Mechanism
Bind 50S → block translocation → bacteriostatic (often bactericidal at high concentrations for some bugs).

## Clinical Importance
- Outpatient CAP when atypical coverage needed
- **Not** for invasive meningococcal disease monotherapy
- QT prolongation interactions (azithromycin generally lower risk than erythromycin)

## AMR Relevance
- *M. abscessus* macrolide inducible resistance (*erm*) — phenotypic testing critical ([[Nontuberculous Mycobacteria]])
- Azithromycin resistance in [[Gonorrhea]] / chlamydia regions — follow guidelines
