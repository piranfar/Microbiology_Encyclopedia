# Antimicrobial Resistance

## One-Sentence Definition

Antimicrobial resistance (AMR) is the ability of a microorganism to survive concentrations of an antimicrobial agent that would normally inhibit or kill it.

## Simple Explanation

The drug stops working — not because the patient changed, but because the microbe did.

## Detailed Scientific Explanation

**Intrinsic vs acquired**
- *Intrinsic:* inherent to the species (e.g., Gram-negative outer membrane excluding vancomycin)
- *Acquired:* new — by [[Mutation and Selection]] or by [[Horizontal Gene Transfer]]

**Where resistance comes from**
1. Chromosomal mutation in a target or regulator
2. Acquisition of a resistance gene on a [[Plasmid]], transposon, or integron
3. Upregulation of intrinsic defences ([[Efflux Pumps]], porin loss)

**Terminology that matters clinically**
- **MDR / XDR / PDR** — multidrug-, extensively-, pan-drug-resistant
- **Tolerance** — survives without growing; MIC unchanged
- **Persistence** — a subpopulation survives; not inherited
- **Heteroresistance** — a resistant subpopulation within a susceptible-looking isolate

Selection pressure is the engine: antimicrobial exposure in humans, animals, and the environment selects for the resistant fraction that already exists. Resistance can carry a fitness cost, but compensatory mutations often erase it — which is why resistance rarely disappears when a drug is withdrawn.

## Mechanism
See [[Mechanisms of Antibiotic Resistance]] for the biochemical categories (enzymatic destruction, target modification, reduced uptake, efflux, bypass).

## Clinical Importance
- Empirical therapy fails; time to effective therapy is a major driver of mortality in [[Sepsis]]
- Drives use of broader, more toxic, more expensive agents
- Some infections are approaching untreatable — carbapenem-resistant Gram-negatives especially

## Research Importance
- Genomic prediction ([[Genotype to Phenotype Prediction]]), new agent discovery ([[AI for Antibiotic Discovery]]), phage therapy, anti-virulence strategies

## Diagnostic Relevance
- Detected phenotypically by [[Antimicrobial Susceptibility Testing]] and genotypically via [[AMR Gene Databases]]

## Public Health
- Global surveillance (WHO GLASS), [[One Health]] reservoirs, [[Antimicrobial Stewardship]], and [[Vaccination]] as prevention
