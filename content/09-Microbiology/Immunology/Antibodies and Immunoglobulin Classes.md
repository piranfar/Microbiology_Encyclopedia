# Antibodies and Immunoglobulin Classes

## One-Sentence Definition

Antibodies (immunoglobulins) are B-cell–derived glycoproteins that bind antigens with specificity — IgM, IgG, IgA, IgE, and IgD differ in structure, distribution, and diagnostic/clinical roles in infectious disease.

## Simple Explanation

Y-shaped proteins that tag microbes for destruction. Different classes work in blood, mucosa, or allergy — labs measure them for diagnosis.

## Detailed Scientific Explanation

| Class | Main site | Microbiology role |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **IgM** | Blood (pentamer) | First response; acute infection serology |
| **IgG** | Blood, tissues | Memory, opsonization, complement activation, placental transfer |
| **IgA** | Mucosa (secretory) | Respiratory/GI/Urogenital defense; breast milk |
| **IgE** | Mast cells/basophils | Parasites; allergic reactions to drugs/organisms |
| **IgD** | B-cell surface | Less central clinically |

**Functions**
- Neutralization (toxins, viruses)
- Opsonization (enhance phagocytosis)
- Complement activation (IgM/IgG)
- ADCC

**Serology interpretation (see [[Serology in Clinical Microbiology]])**
- IgM suggests recent/acute (with caveats)
- IgG may indicate past infection, immunity, or remote exposure
- Fourfold rise in paired sera = evidence of recent infection
- Vaccine-induced IgG (anti-HBs, anti-tetanus) ≠ always disease

## Mechanism
V(D)J recombination → somatic hypermutation → class switching under T-cell help.

## Clinical Importance
- RhIG, IVIG, hyperimmune globulins (rabies, HBV PEP)
- Monoclonal antibody therapeutics (RSV, SARS-CoV-2)
